# Editorial source notes — 22 September 2026

The revision clarifies all 12 timeline summaries, corrects the historical diagram and qualifies research claims in the group work, assignment and semester map. Classroom examples and recommendations are teaching applications, not additional findings attributed to the publications.

## Historical and research claims

- **Bax (2003):** pp. 20–22 distinguish approaches from universal historical phases. Restricted CALL predominates until about 1980; Open CALL continues at the time of writing. Integrated CALL is a goal with limited existing examples. The graphic now has no dated Integrated CALL band. The end of the Open band marks the publication date, not an end to the approach. [Original paper](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/ENL110/callpresentpastandfuture.pdf).
- **Ahmad et al. (1985):** the entry now describes an introduction to computing and CALL for teachers. It no longer presents the book as evidence of a field-wide communicative debate. The authorship is listed in [Ahmad's institutional bibliography](https://www.tcd.ie/Neuroscience/people/kahmad/); the preface and coverage are described in a [contemporary review](https://teslcanadajournal.ca/index.php/tesl/article/download/501/332/528).
- **Effectiveness reviews:** distinguish the populations and findings of [Macaro et al.](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444811000395), [Grgurović et al.](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344013000013) and [Golonka et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/09588221.2012.700315). The Grgurović review includes 37 studies and finds a small positive overall effect; it does not support the assertion that comparison is impossible. [Zhao's 2003 review](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ674877) establishes that CALL meta-analysis predates 2013.
- **Dialogue-based CALL:** section 4.5 of [Bibauw et al.'s accepted manuscript](https://www.serge.bibauw.be/assets/pdf/Bibauw_et_al_2019_Dialogue_based_CALL_pre.pdf) distinguishes handcrafted and probabilistic approaches. The revised lesson does not describe all earlier systems as rule-based or assume direct transfer to general-purpose LLMs.
- **Captioning:** [Montero Perez et al.](https://kulak.kuleuven.be/~u0006844/publications/2013%2C%20monteroperez_vandennoortgate_desmet_system.pdf) report large effects for listening-comprehension and vocabulary outcomes. The lesson asks students to inspect measures and timing before inferring durable unaided listening gains. The claim that this is the strongest evidence in the whole field has been removed.
- **Equity and privacy:** [Ortega (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1558/cj.33855) informs the equity and access discussion. The data-policy questions are explicitly identified as a course extension; answers about a product require its own current policies and terms.

## Scope and checking limits

This was an editorial check of the central claims and bibliographic details, using available original texts, publisher records and supporting author/institutional records. Some full texts were unavailable, and some Crossref requests were rate-limited. The revision does not claim full-text verification of every reference. A DOI confirms the identity of a publication; support for an instructional or research claim must be checked in the publication itself.

## Definition cards and simplified classroom work

- The six definition cards and comparison table distinguish Warschauer and Healey’s three phases from Bax’s three approaches. The cards paraphrase the [Warschauer and Healey author manuscript, pp. 1–2](https://education.uci.edu/uploads/7/2/7/6/72769947/computers_and_language_learning-_an_overview.pdf) and [Bax, pp. 20–24, especially Table 2](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/ENL110/callpresentpastandfuture.pdf). Classroom examples are teaching illustrations. Integrative and Integrated are distinguished explicitly.
- The public class Drive folder was inspected. Group 1’s supplied Chapelle PDF was checked directly: the relevant discussion begins on printed p. 54, Table 3.4 is on p. 55, and explanations extend through p. 58. PDF viewer numbering differs from printed numbering. The group task requires this extract, not all four files in its folder.
- Group 2 selects one of Heil (2016), Rosell-Aguilar (2017) and Loewen et al. (2019). Group 3 selects one of Kohnke et al. (2023), Godwin-Jones (2023) and Warschauer and Ware (2006); the extra 2019 file in Drive is optional. Direct folder links are provided.
- The user confirmed Montero Perez et al. (2013), Winke et al. (2010) and Gilmore (2007) for Group 4, with the meta-analysis as the suggested focus. No Group 4 subfolder was visible during inspection, so the lesson links to the articles instead of inventing a Drive location.
- Group work now asks for one reading summary, one slide/page and a four-minute presentation. Tool demonstrations are optional. Each group has a matching homework path; Path 1 uses six Chapelle criteria and Paths 2–4 use three justified criteria. The checklist is a reference, not an extra requirement to apply seven criteria to every path.
- Criteria adapted for AI writing or multimodal materials and the proposed final-project studies are classroom applications. The lesson distinguishes these from validated frameworks or established findings, and separates inspected design quality, learning outcomes and learning efficiency.
