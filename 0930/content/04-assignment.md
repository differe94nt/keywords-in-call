# One learner. One justified design.

**In your group, choose one anonymous learner from a corpus you can access.** Build a provisional profile and design a short sequence of learning activities for that learner. The sequence needs guided practice and a less-supported task, not a full course. You are proposing instruction from existing evidence; you are not expected to contact the corpus participant or run a learner experiment this week.

**Submit two pages or three slides, plus a small evidence appendix where the corpus terms permit. Prepare a 4-minute explanation.** Use the same group arrangement as last week. No paid AI tool is required.

## 1 · Evidence and learner profile
kind: deliverable
text: Record corpus/version, anonymous learner or script ID, source URL and access date. Use two short samples from that learner where possible; two parts of one interview/script are acceptable if explicitly identified. State task, level/date metadata and sample limits.
- Name one strength and two possible learning priorities, with sample/line IDs and concise evidence.
- For one priority, show a transparent count with a denominator or a documented qualitative comparison. Include successful uses as well as difficulties.
- Separate observed facts, interpretations and unknowns. Label any assumed goal or classroom setting. Do not infer personality, learning style or disability from corpus language.

## 2 · Learning activities and rationale
kind: deliverable
text: Design a 10–15-minute sequence for one of the priorities: a model, supported practice, feedback/revision and a new less-supported task. Explain how your second priority will be monitored or reserved for later.
- State an observable goal and cite one relevant theory/framework or course handout. Explain its connection to the activity rather than listing several theory names.
- Provide actual learner-facing instructions and a small usable example, not just “use AI to practise.” Name the tool and what the learner does with it.
- Specify the scaffold, when it fades, a low-bandwidth alternative and a three-item success checklist aligned with the goal.

## 3 · Check the design
kind: deliverable
text: Walk through the activity as a group. If you use AI, keep the prompt, tool/model label where shown, date and one output excerpt you checked; show one correction or explain why no correction was needed.
- Check a correct answer, a likely learner difficulty and the independent transfer item. A peer walkthrough checks the material, not whether the corpus learner has improved.
- End with one limitation and a future evaluation question. A later final project could compare support conditions, examine feedback uptake or use a delayed transfer task.
- Follow corpus sharing terms. Where quotations are restricted, submit your own analysis and retrievable file/line references instead of copied corpus text. Do not publish or upload restricted scripts by default.

## What a strong submission demonstrates
kind: rubric
text: Four equally weighted criteria, scored 0–2 each (8 points). 0 = missing or unsupported; 1 = partly justified; 2 = explicit and traceable.
- **Evidence:** the same learner is identifiable through anonymous IDs, task context and examples; counting or qualitative analysis is transparent.
- **Profile:** strengths and priorities follow from evidence; unknowns and assumptions are visible.
- **Design:** the learner goal, theory, scaffold and digital affordance fit together; usable learner instructions are supplied.
- **Evaluation:** the independent check measures the stated goal; AI output is reviewed where used and claims stay within the evidence.
