Do this part **without AI**, so you have your own analysis to compare against later. Split the work if you like — two people on writing, two on speaking — but agree the priorities together.

## The analysis, step by step
kind: section
Use line numbers from **The learner data** page so everyone can find what you are talking about.

## Read for meaning first
kind: step
do: Read each sample once without marking anything. What was the learner trying to say? Did she get the message across?
check: Write one sentence per learner: *"She manages to … ."*

## Mark strengths
kind: step
do: Note at least two things each learner does well — vocabulary, structure, organisation, getting her point across, repairing her own speech.

## Identify and describe errors
kind: step
do: Fill in the analysis table below. One row per error. Use your own category names if ours do not fit.
check: For the speaking sample, do **not** count fillers, repetitions or self-corrections as errors. If she corrected herself successfully, that is evidence of knowledge.

## Look for patterns
kind: step
do: Group the rows. Which errors recur? Which block meaning? Which concern the task itself rather than the language?
tip: For the writing sample, re-read the assignment. Did the learner do the two things it explicitly asked for?

## Choose priorities
kind: step
do: Pick **one priority per learner** for the next lesson, and say why it comes first — frequency, effect on meaning, task demands, or how ready she seems to learn it.

## Categories you might use
kind: card
- **Grammar:** articles, verb tense or form, subject–verb agreement, plurals, word order, missing subject
- **Vocabulary:** word choice, word form (*relax / relaxed*), collocation, prepositions
- **Mechanics (writing):** spelling, capital letters, punctuation, spacing
- **Discourse and genre:** paragraphing, linking ideas, register, how the text is organised
- **Task fulfilment:** did the learner do what the task asked?
- **Interaction (speaking):** answering the question, keeping the conversation going, clarity of the message

## Your teaching proposal
kind: section
For each learner, propose a short teaching response to your top priority. Keep it to what you could do in about 15 minutes of class time plus feedback.

## Proposal frame
kind: card
- **Learner and priority:** which learner, which error pattern, which lines show it
- **Goal:** what the learner will be able to do afterwards — observable
- **Activity:** what the learner does, step by step (model → practice → use)
- **Feedback:** what kind of feedback you will give, and when (see the two cards below)
- **Check:** how you will know it worked — on new content, not the same sentences
- **Theory:** one concept from the course that explains your choice (noticing, scaffolding, output…)

## Feedback on writing
kind: card
Ellis's typology separates *how* written feedback is given. Choose on purpose:
- **Direct:** you give the correct form.
- **Indirect:** you show where an error is, but the learner corrects it.
- **Metalinguistic:** a code or a short rule (*"art" = article missing*).
- **Focused or unfocused:** correct one error type, or everything.
refs: ellis2009

## Feedback on speaking
kind: card
Lyster and Ranta classified teachers' oral corrective feedback in classrooms, and found that some types led to more learner *uptake* — learners repairing their own speech — than others:
- **Recast:** you repeat the utterance correctly.
- **Explicit correction:** you say it was wrong and give the right form.
- **Elicitation, clarification request, metalinguistic clue, repetition:** you prompt the learner to correct herself.
refs: lyster_ranta1997
