# Ask the AI — same task, two tools

Now give the AI the work you just did. Use the **free versions**: **Gemini first**, then **Claude or ChatGPT**. Send exactly the same prompts to both, so the comparison is fair.

## Ground rules
kind: card
- **Finish your own analysis first.** If you read the AI's answer first, you will start agreeing with it.
- **A new chat for each sample**, so the writing and speaking analyses do not mix.
- **Same prompt, both tools.** Do not improve the prompt for the second tool — that changes the comparison.
- **Save everything:** tool, the model name the screen shows, the date, your prompt, and the full answer. Paste them into your group document.
- **Free plans have limits.** One person in the group runs each tool, and avoid asking it to "try again" over and over.
- These two samples come from openly licensed, anonymised corpora, so pasting them is allowed. **Never paste your own students' work** into a free AI tool without their agreement.

## The order
kind: section

## Gemini
kind: step
do: Open gemini.google.com with your Google account. Start a new chat. Send Prompt 1 for the writing sample. In a new chat, send Prompt 1 for the speaking sample. Then send Prompt 2 in each chat.

## Claude or ChatGPT
kind: step
do: Choose one: claude.ai or chatgpt.com. Repeat exactly what you did in Gemini — same prompts, same order, new chats.

## The web page
kind: step
do: Choose the teaching plan your group thinks is stronger, go back to that tool, and send Prompt 3. It asks the AI to turn its own plan into an interactive HTML page.
check: Open the page and use it as the learner would — give a right answer, a wrong answer and no answer.

## How to see the HTML page
kind: card
- **Gemini:** choose **Canvas** below the text box before sending Prompt 3, then open **Preview**. If you see no preview, ask for "one single HTML file" and use the ChatGPT method below.
- **Claude:** in **Settings → Capabilities**, turn on **Cloud code execution and file creation**. The page then appears as an artifact beside the chat.
- **ChatGPT (or any tool without a preview):** copy the whole code block → paste it into a plain-text editor (Notepad, or TextEdit set to plain text) → save it as `lesson.html` → open the file in Chrome.

## The prompts
kind: section
Use the **Copy** buttons on **The learner data** page to copy each sample exactly. Replace the parts in [square brackets].

## Prompt 1 · Writing analysis
kind: prompt
```text
You are an experienced EFL writing teacher. Analyse the learner text below.

About the learner: first language Chinese; upper-intermediate English class (about CEFR B1+/B2) in a university intensive English program.
The assignment she was given: "Write the first draft of your problem/solution essay and submit it here. Use the essay plan on p.135 of your textbook to help you. Remember: each solution should have its own paragraph. Don't forget to include either one quotation OR one paraphrased sentence from an outside source."

Learner text (line numbers in my copy start at 1 with the title "Problem about sleep"):
[PASTE SAMPLE A HERE]

Please give:
1. Two or three things the learner does well.
2. A table of errors with columns: line number | what she wrote | a possible correction | category | global or local (does it block meaning?) | short explanation.
3. Patterns: which errors recur?
4. Task fulfilment: did she do what the assignment asked? Be specific.
5. The two most important priorities for her next lesson, with reasons.
6. Anything you are unsure about.
Do not rewrite the whole text.
```

## Prompt 1 · Speaking analysis
kind: prompt
```text
You are an experienced EFL speaking teacher. Analyse the learner's speech in the interview transcript below.

About the learner: Japanese adult learner, rated level 4 of 9 on the ACTFL-ALC Standard Speaking Test. Task: describe a picture of a neighbourhood on a sunny Sunday morning, then answer follow-up questions. The picture is not available.

Transcript notation: (um) = filler; (.) and (..) = pauses; word- = repeated or broken-off fragment; {the} = the learner started this, then corrected herself and said what follows; [laughter] = non-verbal sound; … = unfinished sentence. Analyse only the lines starting "Learner:". Lines are numbered from 1 in my copy.

[PASTE SAMPLE B HERE]

Please give:
1. Two or three things the learner does well as a speaker.
2. A table of errors with columns: line number | what she said | a possible correction | category | global or local | short explanation.
3. Patterns: which errors recur?
4. The two most important priorities for her next lesson, with reasons.
5. Anything you are unsure about.
This is spoken language: do not treat fillers, pauses, repetitions or self-corrections as errors. You only have a transcript, so make no claims about pronunciation.
```

## Prompt 2 · Teaching plan
kind: prompt
Send this in the same chat, after Prompt 1.
```text
Based on your analysis, choose ONE priority for this learner and design a 15-minute teaching response for a small class of similar learners.
Include:
1. The goal — what the learner will be able to do afterwards.
2. The activity, step by step: a short model or explanation, guided practice, then a task where she uses the language more freely.
3. The type of feedback you will give and why (for example recast, elicitation, direct or indirect correction).
4. How you will check, with new content, whether it worked.
5. One learning theory or research finding that supports your design. Name the author and year only if you are sure the source exists; otherwise describe the idea without a citation.
```

## Prompt 3 · Turn the plan into a web page
kind: prompt
Send this in the chat whose teaching plan your group chose.
```text
Turn your teaching plan into a single, self-contained interactive HTML page that the learner can use on her own.
The page should include:
1. A short title and the learning goal in learner-friendly English.
2. A mini-lesson: a clear explanation with 2–3 new example sentences (not her original sentences).
3. Practice items (5–8) with immediate feedback that explains why an answer is right or wrong, and an optional hint before the answer.
4. A final task with new content and no hints.
5. A short "For the teacher" section at the bottom with the answer key.
Requirements: one HTML file; no external libraries, images or links; no AI features; no collection or storage of learner data; large readable text; works with keyboard only; does not rely on colour alone.
After the code, list the steps you used to check that every answer key is correct.
```

## Optional · Ask the AI to audit itself
kind: prompt
```text
Look back at your error table. Which of your identified "errors" could be acceptable English in this context? Which of your explanations about why she made the error are guesses? Which important problem might you have missed? Answer honestly and briefly.
```
