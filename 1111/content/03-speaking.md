# Speaking

Learners need to speak a lot to learn to speak — and many hesitate to. Research asks two questions you can design for: **what kind of feedback makes learners try again**, and **what practice space lowers the barrier to speaking**. Recent studies test AI chatbots as that space, including with young learners.

## What research says
kind: section

## Feedback that makes learners try again
kind: paper
badge: Classic · 1997
did: An analysis of 18.3 hours of interaction in four primary-level French immersion classrooms, classifying six types of teacher feedback and how learners responded to each.
found: Teachers overwhelmingly used **recasts** (repeating the learner's sentence correctly), yet recasts were the least likely to lead learners to repair their own speech. **Elicitation, metalinguistic clues, clarification requests and repetition** led to learner-generated repair more often.
means: If your activity includes a feedback partner — human or AI — ask it to prompt the learner to try again, not just to say the correct form.
refs: lyster1997

## Text chat as rehearsal for speech
kind: paper
badge: Classic · 2002
did: A classroom experiment in which the experimental group spent two of four weekly contact hours in a text chatroom, grounded in models of speech production and working memory.
found: The chatroom group developed **more oral proficiency** than the control group.
means: Typing in real time can rehearse the same processes as speaking. A chat round before a speaking round gives hesitant learners time to plan.
refs: payne2002

## An AI partner for speaking practice
kind: paper
badge: Recent · 2024
did: EFL learners did interactive speaking activities with an AI chatbot (Andy); a comparison group did conventional face-to-face speaking activities with peers. The study measured speaking performance and willingness to communicate, and asked learners about the experience.
found: The AI-mediated activities were **more effective for speaking performance and willingness to communicate**, and learners viewed them positively.
means: An AI partner can lower the barrier to speaking. It was compared with peer practice, so plan how AI practice leads back to talking with people.
refs: fathi2024

## Chatbots with young learners — alone or in pairs
kind: paper
badge: Recent · 2024
did: 85 elementary EFL learners in a three-week summer programme (45 minutes a day) practised with a generative-AI chatbot called CoolE Bot, either individually or in pairs; a third group had conventional instruction. Researchers based in Taiwan.
found: **Both chatbot groups outperformed** the conventional group on speaking tests, with **no significant difference between individual and paired** use. Learners reported enjoyment, more confidence and less anxiety; pairs also helped each other.
means: Sharing one device between two learners works — useful when devices are limited, and it adds peer support. The study used one specific chatbot; general-purpose tools such as ChatGPT or Claude have age limits, so check them before using a chatbot with children.
refs: tai2024

## Activity ideas
kind: section

## Rehearse with AI, perform with people
kind: activity
do:
- Pairs share one device and practise a role-play with an AI partner (ordering food, asking for directions, a club interview).
- Swap partners and perform the same role-play **without** the AI.
- The listener asks one follow-up question the AI did not ask.
role: The AI plays the other speaker and keeps the conversation going with short questions.
check: Who talks more — the AI or the learner? Limit the AI to one or two sentences per turn. For learners under 18, check the tool's age rules (see the AI studio).
refs: tai2024 | fathi2024

## Chat first, then talk
kind: activity
do:
- Five minutes of text chat on the topic in pairs or small groups.
- Five minutes of spoken discussion on the same topic, without the chat window.
- Learners note one phrase they used in both.
role: AI suggests the discussion questions and a short list of useful phrases at the learners' level.
check: The spoken round must be new talk, not reading the chat aloud.
refs: payne2002

## A partner who prompts, not corrects
kind: activity
do:
- Write the AI's instructions: when the learner makes an error, ask a clarification question or give a clue — do not give the correct sentence.
- Learners talk with the AI, then note when they corrected themselves.
- Compare with a version where the AI simply restates the correct form.
role: The AI acts as the conversation partner with your feedback rules.
check: Does the AI actually follow the rule? Language models often slip back into correcting.
refs: lyster1997

## Mistakes to avoid
kind: card
- The AI talks more than the learner.
- Correcting every error and stopping the conversation.
- Measuring only enjoyment, not what learners can say afterwards.
- Giving young learners general-purpose AI tools that are 18+ only.
