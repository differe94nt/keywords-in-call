# Writing

Feedback is the centre of writing instruction — and the oldest argument in the field is whether correcting grammar helps at all. AI now produces feedback instantly, which makes the real question sharper: **what do learners do with the feedback**, and does improving *this* draft mean they write better *next* time?

## What research says
kind: section

## The case against grammar correction
kind: paper
badge: Classic · 1996
did: A position paper reviewing research and theory on correcting grammar in L2 writing classes.
found: Truscott argued that grammar correction **should be abandoned**: research showed it ineffective, there were theoretical and practical reasons to expect that, and it can do harm. The paper started a long-running debate in the field.
means: Do not assume more correction is better. Decide what your feedback is for, how much, and what learners must do with it.
refs: truscott1996

## Automated writing evaluation in the classroom
kind: paper
badge: Classic · 2006
did: A review of automated writing evaluation (AWE): how the systems work, what research had found, and a proposed agenda for studying how AWE is actually used in classrooms.
found: Scoring engines had been studied psychometrically, but **little research looked at classroom use** and its results. The authors called for research on both the writing process and the products.
means: The useful question is not "is the machine's score accurate?" but "how will learners and teachers use its feedback?"
refs: warschauer2006

## Teacher feedback or ChatGPT feedback?
kind: paper
badge: Recent · 2026
did: 20 Chinese undergraduates wrote argumentative essays, received feedback from both their teacher and ChatGPT, and revised. The researchers analysed the revisions and surveyed the students.
found: Students **engaged more and revised more accurately with teacher feedback**, especially on language and content — but they were particularly successful at using **ChatGPT's feedback on organisation**. They preferred teacher feedback overall while recognising ChatGPT's strengths.
means: Use AI feedback as a complement, and give each source the job it does best.
refs: zou2026

## Working through AI feedback alone or together
kind: paper
badge: Recent · 2024
did: A seven-week experiment with 117 second-year EFL students and six teachers at a Chinese university. Students processed ChatGPT feedback individually, or together with a teacher, with peers, or with both.
found: Students who worked through the feedback **individually improved their drafts the most** — but those who worked through it **with their teacher did best on a new, similar writing task**.
means: A better draft is not the same as learning. If you want learners to write better next time, build in teacher-guided discussion of the AI feedback and check with a new task.
refs: yan2024

## Activity ideas
kind: section

## Two sources, one decision
kind: activity
do:
- Learners receive teacher or peer feedback **and** AI feedback on the same paragraph.
- In a table, they decide for each suggestion: accept, change or reject — and why.
- They revise, then explain one decision to a partner.
role: AI gives feedback using criteria you set (organisation, ideas, language).
check: Which source did learners trust, and were they right to?
refs: zou2026

## Discuss the feedback, then write something new
kind: activity
do:
- Groups read AI feedback on a sample paragraph with the teacher guiding the discussion.
- Each learner writes a **new** short paragraph on a different topic, without AI.
- Compare: which feedback points show up in the new paragraph?
role: AI produces the feedback on the sample paragraph.
check: The new paragraph — not the revised one — shows what was learned.
refs: yan2024

## One error pattern, indirect feedback
kind: activity
do:
- Tell the AI to give feedback on **one** pattern only (for example verb forms), marking where the problem is without correcting it.
- Learners correct the marked places themselves, then check with a partner.
- A short new piece of writing tests the same pattern.
role: AI marks the pattern according to your rules.
check: Did the AI stick to one pattern, and did it avoid giving the answers?
refs: truscott1996 | warschauer2006

## Mistakes to avoid
kind: card
- Letting the AI rewrite the whole text — then nobody knows what the learner can do.
- Judging success only by the revised draft.
- Feedback on everything at once.
- Pasting learners' writing into free AI tools without their agreement.

## Revisit your draft
kind: questions
- Who gives feedback in your activity, on what, and when?
- What must learners *do* with the feedback?
- How will you check learning with a new piece of writing?
