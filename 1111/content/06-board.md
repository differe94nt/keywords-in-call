# Activity board

Forty small activity types — ten for each skill — and nine websites and AI tools that can run them. **Pick a tool, then click every activity it could serve.** One tool can fit several activities, and one activity can combine several tools. Your matches stay in this browser; copy them into your group document before you leave.

## Four steps

## Look at all the activities in your skill
kind: step
do: Use the skill buttons to show your skill. Read all ten — the code (L1, S4 …) is how you will name your activity later.

## Try the websites and AI tools
kind: step
do: Open each tool below the board and try it for two minutes. Read **Before you use it** on each tool card.

## Match tools to activities
kind: step
do: Choose a tool, then click each activity it could serve. The same tool may fit several activities; you may combine tools for one activity. Match **at least five activities** with at least one tool each.

## Choose your group's activity
kind: step
do: Fill in the worksheet under the board for the **one** activity your group will build and show. You will start your presentation with it.

## learnyourway
kind: tool
mono: LY
name: Learn Your Way
url: https://learnyourway.withgoogle.com/
by: Google Research experiment
what: Turns textbook material into several formats — immersive text with embedded questions, narrated slides, an audio lesson, a mind map and quizzes. The demos are school-subject lessons (for example science and economics) in English.
mind: You mainly work with Google's demo lessons; check whether adding your own material is open. It is content-subject English, not an English course — choose a lesson your learners could follow.
model: L1 | L9 | R3 | R8
why: The audio lesson suits gist and detail listening and mind-mapping; the immersive text with questions suits skim–scan–probe; the mind map is a ready-made graphic organiser to compare with learners' own.

## lll
kind: tool
mono: LL
name: Little Language Lessons
url: https://labs.google/lll/en
by: Google Labs experiment (Gemini)
what: Three small experiments: **Tiny Lesson** gives words, phrases and tips for a situation you type; **Slang Hang** generates an informal conversation; **Word Cam** labels objects in a photo with words in the target language.
mind: Everything is AI-generated — check phrases and slang before teaching them. Check that your learners' language pair is offered.
model: S3 | S8 | R9 | W9
why: Tiny Lesson prepares phrases for a speech-act scenario; Word Cam gives vocabulary for a picture prompt; Slang Hang supplies informal language to contrast with a formal register.

## pronounce
kind: tool
mono: Pr
name: Pronounce
url: https://www.getpronounce.com/
by: Commercial AI speaking coach (not a Google product)
what: Learners record themselves; the app analyses pronunciation and clarity, points to sounds and words to practise, and offers AI conversation coaches in several accents.
mind: Needs an account; the free plan is limited. It uploads learners' voice recordings to a company — get consent, and do not use it with children's voices.
model: S1 | S6 | S9 | S10 | L7
why: Private recording with feedback before speaking to a partner; targeted sound practice; timed talks; coaches with different accents for comparison.

## quizlet
kind: tool
mono: Qz
name: Quizlet
url: https://quizlet.com/latest
by: Commercial flashcard platform
what: Teacher- or student-made card sets with audio, and study modes such as Flashcards, Learn, Test and Match.
mind: Some study modes are limited on the free plan. Card audio is text-to-speech — check minimal pairs by ear before using them.
model: L3 | R9 | W5
why: Audio cards for hearing pairs of words; definition-and-example cards for vocabulary in context; "error → fix rule" cards for an error corpus.

## wordwall
kind: tool
mono: Ww
name: Wordwall
url: https://wordwall.net/
by: Commercial activity maker
what: Turns your content into games and printables from templates such as Quiz, Match up, Flashcards and Spin the wheel.
mind: The free Basic plan allows 3 activities and 12 templates (check the price page). Ready-made community activities vary in quality.
model: R5 | R9 | W5 | S5 | S9
why: Match up for headlines and paragraphs; quizzes for "which sentence is correct?"; Spin the wheel to pick debate or pitch topics.

## wordcoach
kind: tool
mono: WC
name: Google Word Coach
url: https://www.google.com/search?q=google+word+coach
by: Vocabulary game inside Google Search
what: Short rounds of five questions on word meanings, synonyms, antonyms and pictures.
mind: The official game runs inside Google Search (search "Google Word Coach", easiest on a phone). The *googlewordscoach.com* site in the handout is an unofficial fan site.
model: R9
why: A two-minute vocabulary warm-up; follow it with an activity where learners use the words.

## semantris
kind: tool
mono: Se
name: Semantris
url: https://research.google.com/semantris/
by: Google Research word-association game
what: Type a clue word; a language model decides which words on screen are most related. *Arcade* is fast and timed; *Blocks* is slower.
mind: The model judges how related words are, not whether the English is correct. Use it to talk about meaning, then check with a dictionary.
model: R8 | R9
why: After a few rounds, learners build a word web of related terms — a graphic organiser — and explain the links.

## artsculture
kind: tool
mono: AC
name: The Lab · Google Arts & Culture
url: https://artsandculture.google.com/lab
by: Google Arts & Culture experiments
what: A collection of experiments. **Say What You See** shows an image; learners type a description of up to 120 characters, and the AI generates an image from it to compare with the original.
mind: Say What You See is typed — for speaking, have learners describe aloud first. It teaches image prompting, so precise nouns and adjectives score well.
model: S3 | W3 | W6
why: A picture prompt to describe and compare; a very short write with an instant visual check; compressing several details into one precise sentence.
link: [Open Say What You See](https://artsandculture.google.com/experiment/say-what-you-see/jwG3m7wQShZngw)

## englinker
kind: tool
mono: En
name: Englinker
url: https://englinker.com/about/
by: Independent site run by one founder
what: A curated directory of English-learning resources — books and documents, games, tools and AI, videos and podcasts — with study guides. It is a list of links, not an exercise site.
mind: It describes itself as a first version. Check each resource it links to before using it with learners.
model: L1 | L7
why: Its videos and podcasts section is a quick place to find clips for gist-and-detail listening and for comparing accents.

## Theory tags
kind: tags
- i+1 — input a little beyond learners' current level
- ZPD/Scaf — support first, then remove it so learners can do it alone
- Bandura — learning by watching a model
- Behav — practice, repetition and reinforcement
- Swain — producing language makes learners notice gaps
- Piaget — reorganising what you know when new information does not fit
- UDL — several ways to take in, engage with and show learning
