# Present and reflect

Each group teaches its activity to the class — your classmates are the learners. Then everyone reflects, using the same headings as the portfolio.

## Your presentation · 6 minutes plus 2 for feedback
kind: section
Open your page and tools **before** your turn. Four groups take 32 minutes, leaving 8 for switching. Start with one line: *"Our activity is [code and name from the Activity board], using [our page / the tools we chose]."*

## Our learners and goal
kind: step
time: 30 seconds
do: Who the activity is for, and what they will be able to do afterwards.

## From first idea to revised plan
kind: step
time: 1 minute
do: Show version 0 next to version 1. Name **one study** that changed your design, and what it changed.

## Teach it
kind: step
time: 3 minutes
do: Run the activity live with the class as learners. Let them actually do it — do not just show screens.

## What the AI got right and wrong
kind: step
time: 30 seconds
do: One thing the AI built well, one thing you had to fix, and how you found the problem.

## What we would test with real learners
kind: step
time: 1 minute
do: Classmates trying it shows whether it is usable — not that your learners would learn from it. What evidence would show that it works with them, on new material, and what result would make you change it?

## Peer feedback
kind: section
Each listener answers these for every group — on paper, in the chat, or in a shared form. The group keeps the answers: they go into **Feedback I received** in each member's portfolio.

## For each group
kind: questions
- As a learner, what did you actually do — and what did you learn?
- Which design decision is best supported by the research they cited?
- Where did the activity drift from its learning goal?
- One thing to keep, and one thing to change before using it with real learners.

## Reflection · for your portfolio
kind: section
Answer individually. Each set matches a section of the portfolio, so your answers can go straight into it.

## Where I started — a note
kind: card
Version 0 is where **today's** activity started. Your portfolio's *Where I started* goes back to your beliefs at the start of the semester; use version 0 as one example inside it, not instead of it.

## Where I started
kind: questions
- What was your group's first idea (version 0)?
- What did you assume about the learners, the skill or the AI before reading?

## What I learned
kind: questions
- Which study changed your thinking most, and how?
- What did you learn about your chosen skill that you did not know this morning?
- Which finding surprised you — in your skill or another group's?

## What I tried
kind: questions
- What did you ask the AI to build, and what came back the first time?
- What did you have to fix, and how did you find the problem?
- What would you do differently when guiding an AI next time?

## Evidence of my learning
kind: questions
- Which artifact shows your learning best: version 0 and version 1, a prompt revision, the tested activity, or the peer feedback?
- What exact before-and-after change can you point to?
- What was your own contribution, and what does this evidence still not show?

## Feedback I received
kind: questions
- What was the most useful comment from a classmate?
- What will you change because of it?

## Looking ahead
kind: questions
- Could this activity become part of your final project — as a teaching demo, or as something to test in research?
- What would you need to know about real learners before using it?

## Before you leave
kind: checklist
- Version 0, version 1 and the final activity are saved in the group document
- The version log is filled in
- Peer feedback collected for our group
- My reflection answers are saved for the portfolio
- I wrote down my own contribution, and which AI tools I used and how I checked their output (for my AI-use statement)
- Today's work is one portfolio artifact; the portfolio still needs its other artifacts and tools from the semester
