# Reading Vahid Aryadoust

This week we read one researcher closely. Vahid Aryadoust works on **language assessment**, but his studies reach every theme of this semester: **teaching, testing, materials design, the four skills and AI**. Reading fifteen of his papers side by side shows how one research agenda moves from *what are we measuring?* to *can AI help us measure and teach it?*

## Why his work matters
kind: section
Three lines of influence run through the papers this week.

## 1 · Mapping a field before studying it
kind: card
His **scientometric reviews** use citation networks to show what a field has studied and where the gaps are — for comprehension subskills, for CALL, for automated feedback. They are a model for the literature review in your own final project.

## 2 · Asking what a test really measures
kind: card
Much of his work asks whether scores mean what we think: **validity arguments**, **construct definitions** of listening, the effect of **unreliable measures** on research results. The same questions apply to any quiz, rubric or AI score you design.

## 3 · Testing AI, not trusting it
kind: card
His recent studies put generative AI to work — writing listening tests, scoring EAP speaking, giving formative feedback to children — and then check the output with the same measurement tools used for human raters and items.
