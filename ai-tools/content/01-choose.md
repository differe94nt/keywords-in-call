# Start from the job, not the tool

This studio collects the AI tools you can use to **build** teaching materials, **show** them to an audience, and **organise** the literature behind them. Every tool fact here links to the company's own help page, checked in October 2026 — products change quickly, so check again before you depend on one.

**One rule from Week 4 still applies:** decide what learners need to *do*, then choose the tool. A polished output is not evidence of learning.

## Four habits for every tool
kind: card
- **Specify before you generate.** Learners, level, goal, theory, length, format — the more precise the request, the less you fix later.
- **Test like a learner.** Try a wrong answer, an empty answer and a slow connection before anyone else sees it.
- **Check every fact and answer key.** Generated material can be fluent and wrong.
- **Keep a record.** Save the prompt, the tool, the date and what you changed — it belongs in your portfolio and your proposal.

## Questions before you choose
kind: questions
- Who will use the tool — you while preparing, or learners during the lesson?
- How old are those users, and is the tool allowed for them?
- What does the tool do that a printed handout or a partner could not?
- What will you do if it is unavailable on the day?
- Where does the data go — your learners' writing, voices or names?
