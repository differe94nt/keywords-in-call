# The tools

Each card says what the tool is good for, how to start, what to watch for, and its **age and access rules** — which matter as soon as your learners are under 18. Facts were checked on the companies' help pages in October 2026.

## For reading and organising sources
kind: section

## Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)
kind: tool
badge: Sources → answers with citations
best: Organising a literature review. It answers only from the sources you add, and every claim links back to the passage it came from.
start: Open notebook.google.com → **Create new notebook** → **Add sources**. The old notebooklm.google.com address now redirects here. Full walkthrough on the **Gemini Notebook manual** page.
limit: It cannot judge whether a source is good, and it only knows what you uploaded. YouTube adds only the transcript; web pages add only their text; paywalled pages are not supported.
access: The free plan allows 100 notebooks and 50 sources per notebook. Your university account may have it switched off — try a personal Google account if so.
link: [Open Gemini Notebook](https://notebook.google.com/) · [Plans and limits](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16213268?hl=en)

## For building materials
kind: section

## Gems in Gemini
kind: tool
badge: A reusable assistant with your rules
best: Making the same kind of material again and again — "a worksheet designer for A2 learners that always adds an answer key" — without retyping your instructions.
start: gemini.google.com → **Gems** → **New Gem** → give it a name and instructions → optionally add files under **Knowledge** (you can add a Gemini Notebook) → **Save**.
limit: Instructions shape the output; they do not guarantee it. Check every result.
access: Personal accounts: 13 or over (or the age required in your country).
link: [Gems help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15146780?hl=en)

## Gemini Canvas
kind: tool
badge: Documents, slides, web apps, quizzes
best: Turning a lesson idea into something you can open and use — a worksheet, an interactive web page, a simple game, a quiz, an infographic — then editing it directly or by asking for changes.
start: gemini.google.com → under the text box choose **Canvas** → describe what you want. Use **Share & export** to share a link or export to Google Docs or Slides.
limit: Shared links open on gemini.google.com, not in the Gemini mobile app. Test every interactive element yourself.
access: You must be 18 or over to add AI features to an app made in Canvas.
link: [Canvas help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16047321?hl=en)

## Claude Artifacts
kind: tool
badge: Interactive pages next to the chat
best: Self-contained interactive pages — a practice activity, a game, a presentation of your materials, your portfolio — that you can test right away and then save as an HTML file.
start: claude.ai → describe the activity and ask for it as an interactive artifact. The prompts on **Build with an LLM** work well here.
limit: Generated code and answer keys need testing. Ask for a version that makes no AI calls and collects no data if learners will use it.
access: Claude accounts are 18+ only, so this is a teacher-side tool for school-age learners.
link: [Artifacts help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/17153992-what-are-artifacts-and-how-do-i-use-them) · [Age requirement](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13117299-minimum-age-requirement-access-restriction)

## Google AI Studio · Build mode
kind: tool
badge: Prompt → working web app
best: Building your own small teaching tool from a description, then changing it by chatting or by pointing at the part of the screen you want changed (annotation mode).
start: aistudio.google.com → **Build** → describe the app. It creates a full web app you can preview and deploy.
limit: The free tier's terms say human reviewers may read what you submit and that you should not submit personal information. Do not paste learner data.
access: **18+ only, and the terms forbid using it in an app "directed towards or likely to be accessed by" anyone under 18.** An app that calls Gemini live cannot be given to school-age learners. Workspace for Education users under 18 are blocked.
link: [Build mode docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/aistudio-build-mode) · [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)

## ChatGPT
kind: tool
badge: General assistant · Study mode
best: Drafting and revising materials, generating example texts at a set level, and — with Study mode — tutoring-style practice that asks before it tells.
start: chatgpt.com → describe the material using the prompts on **Build with an LLM**.
limit: Can still give direct answers or make mistakes in Study mode. Check output before use.
access: 13 or over; users under 18 need a parent or guardian's permission.
link: [Age guidance](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8313401-is-chatgpt-safe-for-all-ages) · [Study mode](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11780217-using-study-mode-in-chatgpt)

## For showing materials and live interaction
kind: section

## Gamma
kind: tool
badge: AI slide decks
best: A first draft of a presentation from a prompt, an outline, or an uploaded document — quick for showing a lesson plan or project proposal.
start: gamma.app → create a presentation from text or a file → edit the result.
limit: Generated decks tend to look alike and can add claims you did not make. Free-plan limits apply; check the current plan before relying on it.
access: Check the terms for your account type.
link: [Gamma presentations](https://gamma.app/products/presentations)

## Canva
kind: tool
badge: AI presentations and visual design
best: Visual handouts, posters and slides, with AI that drafts slides from a prompt.
start: canva.com → create a presentation with Canva AI → describe the topic.
limit: Free AI uses are limited. Check image licences before reusing images outside Canva.
access: Check the terms for your account type.
link: [Create a presentation with Canva AI](https://www.canva.com/help/using-magic-presentations/)

## Kahoot! AI question generator
kind: tool
badge: Live quiz games
best: A quick, energetic check of understanding in class. It can generate questions from a topic, a PDF or a web page.
start: Create a kahoot → use the AI question generator → edit every question and answer.
limit: Generated questions often test recall only. Rewrite at least some to test the learning goal. Some AI features may need a paid plan.
access: Players join with a PIN and do not need accounts.
link: [Generate a kahoot with AI](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/articles/40803785990675-How-to-generate-a-kahoot-with-AI)

## A plain baseline
kind: card
Not every lesson needs AI. A Google Form, a printed handout or a partner activity is often the better choice — especially with young learners or unreliable internet. In your proposal, explain what the AI tool adds over this baseline.
