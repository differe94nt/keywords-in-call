# Step 3 · Compare and contrast

Two keywords rarely disagree about facts. They disagree about what counts as the unit, the method, and the evidence — which is why the same study can support both.

Tick the keywords you want side by side. The table is built from the cards in Step 2, so editing a card updates this.

## Compared with what?
keywords: CALL | TELL | MALL | DGBLL
why: Every "it works" claim needs a baseline, and these four keywords hide four different baselines.
- CALL studies compare technology with no technology. What else changed at the same time?
- TELL puts "enhanced" in the name. Enhanced relative to which lesson, taught by whom?
- MALL trials often compare an app with nothing at all. Would paper flashcards have done the same work?
- DGBLL compares play with instruction, and then reports motivation. Is motivation the outcome or the mechanism?
- Bring back one sentence: for one of your studies, name the comparison group and what it did instead.

## Who is in control of the feedback?
keywords: AWE | GenAI | Autonomy | CMC
why: All four put a response in front of the learner. They differ on who decides what the response is worth.
- AWE gives a judgement the learner did not ask for. GenAI gives whatever the learner's prompt invited. Does that change what gets learned?
- A tool that never corrects you is comfortable. Which of these four risks that most?
- Autonomy research assumes learners can evaluate feedback. Which of your studies tested that assumption rather than assuming it?
- In CMC the feedback comes from a person who can be wrong too. Is that better or only different?
- Bring back one sentence: who decided what counted as an error in your study?

## What counts as evidence here?
keywords: Multimodal | CMC | GenAI | TPACK
why: These four sit at different distances from the lab, so their strongest evidence looks nothing alike.
- Multimodal research can run an eye-tracking experiment. GenAI research mostly cannot yet. What follows for how confidently each can claim a cause?
- CMC analyses transcripts; TPACK analyses questionnaires. Which claim would you defend to a sceptical examiner?
- One of your keywords leans on preprints. What would you check before citing one?
- Perception and performance come apart in several of your studies. Where exactly?
- Bring back one sentence: name the strongest single piece of evidence in your five, and say why it is the strongest.
