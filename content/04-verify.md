# Step 4 · Checking what the AI gave you

A summary can be fluent, well organised, correctly formatted — and wrong. Fluency is produced by the same process whether the papers exist or not.

Work through the seven checks on one summary. Tick a box only when you can point at the evidence on screen. Use the tracer below to look up any citation in one click.

## 1 · The paper exists
do: Copy the exact title into Crossref or Scholar — never back into the chatbot that produced it. Confirm authors, year and journal all at once.
fail: Real authors, real journal, plausible title, nothing indexed. The most common failure, and the easiest to catch.

## 2 · The link resolves to that paper
do: Open the DOI. Check the title on the publisher page matches the citation you were given, word for word.
fail: A DOI that resolves to a different article. A borrowed identifier is still a fabrication.

## 3 · The claim is on a page
do: Pick three sentences from the summary at random. Find the page in the PDF that supports each. Write down the page number.
fail: The claim is in the paper's introduction, describing someone else's study, and has been reported as this paper's finding.

## 4 · The numbers are the study's own
do: Check every effect size, sample size and percentage against the results section, not the abstract.
fail: Numbers that are plausible, rounded and absent from the paper. Invented figures read exactly like real ones.

## 5 · The conditions survive
do: Name the learners, the length and the measure for each finding. Keep "for beginners", "over eight weeks", "on an immediate post-test".
fail: A finding that held on an immediate post-test only, restated as if it held in general.

## 6 · The disagreements are visible
do: Ask where your five studies conflict, then look for that conflict in the summary.
fail: Five studies flattened into one agreeable consensus. Every sentence can be true and the summary still wrong.

## 7 · Nothing important is missing
do: Ask what a specialist on this keyword would expect to see cited. Search for it deliberately.
fail: The landmark study is absent and nothing marks the absence. Fluent text has no gaps in it.

## Red flags
kind: flags
- No author names anywhere in the summary, only "studies show" and "research indicates".
- Every study agrees, and the agreement is stated more strongly than any single paper does.
- A citation you cannot find in two databases.
- Numbers without a sample size attached.
- Verbs that describe activity rather than results: explores, investigates, examines, highlights.
- Vocabulary grander than the findings: "cushions against decay", "threshold moderator".
- A claim about teachers or workload in a set of five studies that only tested learners.
- The summary would fit any five papers on the topic, including papers you have not read.
