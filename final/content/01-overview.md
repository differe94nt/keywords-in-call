# One project, two ways to do it

Your final project can take one of two forms. **Research** asks a question about digital materials or AI in language teaching and answers it with data. **Teaching demo** designs a lesson for real learners and teaches 20 minutes of it to us. Both start from the same habits you have practised all semester: know the learner, read the literature, justify the design, check the evidence.

The portfolio is individual.

## Two tracks side by side
kind: section

| | Research | Teaching demo |
|---|---|---|
| **The question it answers** | What happens when learners use this material or tool? | How should this lesson be designed for these learners, and why? |
| **Midterm proposal** | Literature review (5 sources) · at least 1 research question · method (quantitative or qualitative) | Literature review (5 sources) supporting your design · target learner analysis · 20-minute teaching plan · how you will integrate AI |
| **Final presentation** | Literature review (5) · research question(s) · method · results and discussion (at least pilot results) | Target learner analysis · literature review (5) · teaching plan outline · 20-minute teaching demonstration |
| **Where AI fits** | AI can be the *topic* (studying a tool) or a *helper* (organising literature, analysing data) — never the evidence | AI can build materials, run an activity, or support practice — you justify each use |
| **You need** | Participants you can actually reach, an instrument, a way to analyse data | A real or clearly specified learner group, materials you have tested, a backup without internet |

## Which track suits you?
kind: questions
- Do you want to *find something out* (research) or *make something work in a classroom* (teaching demo)?
- Can you reach learners or participants before 12/16? Research needs data; a teaching demo needs a learner group you can describe precisely.
- Is there a tool or material you already use that you would like to test, or improve?

## Topics from your individual meetings
kind: card

- Multimodal vocabulary teaching — images, sound and text together
- Learners with hearing impairments — visual access, captions, sign-supported materials
