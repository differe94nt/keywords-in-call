# Pitch your idea, find partners

Today you sell your idea in about one minute — an **elevator pitch** — and listen to others do the same. By the end of class, everyone has a track and a working title. Teaching demos are done in groups; research can be done in a group or on your own.

**Two circles.** Teaching-demo people sit together; research people sit together. If you are undecided, start in one circle and move after the first round.

## How the session runs
kind: section

## Prepare your pitch
kind: step
time: 5 minutes, alone
do: Fill in the pitch frame for your track below. Write it, then say it aloud once to yourself. Aim for 60–90 seconds.

## Round 1 · pitch and listen
kind: step
time: About 20 minutes
do: Each person pitches. Listeners write one question and one suggestion on a sticky note or in the chat, and hand it to the speaker.

## Round 2 · ask as many questions as possible
kind: step
time: About 15 minutes
do: Pick two or three pitches that interest the circle. Listeners ask every question they can think of — use the question banks below. The speaker answers briefly or writes "don't know yet".
check: A question you can't answer yet is useful: it tells you what to find out next.

## Form groups or confirm a solo project
kind: step
time: About 10 minutes
do: **Teaching demo:** join people with a **shared learner group** (for example young learners) or a **shared question** (for example multimodal vocabulary). **Research:** join others with a shared question, or confirm that you will work on your own. Agree on a working title and one next step.
check: Before you leave, every group and every individual researcher submits the exit ticket.

## Pitch frame · teaching demo
kind: prompt
Answer three questions: **who** the learners are, **why** they need this lesson, and **how** AI may help.
```text
My learners are … (age, level, setting, one detail that matters).
They need … because … (the gap or goal you noticed).
In my 20-minute lesson they will … (what learners actually do).
AI could help by … — and I will check it by …
I am looking for partners who … (interest, skill, learner group).
```

## Pitch frame · research
kind: prompt
Sell the idea: your **research question**, your **method**, and **how it fits the course focus**.
```text
I want to find out whether / how … (research question, one sentence).
The participants would be … and I can reach them through …
I would collect … (test, questionnaire, interview, recordings) and analyse it by …
It fits the course focus because it is about … (digital material design / AI in language teaching).
The part I am least sure about is …
```

## Questions for teaching-demo pitches
kind: questions
- Who exactly are your learners? How old, what level, what setting?
- Why do these learners need this — what did you notice, or what do they say?
- What will learners *do* in the 20 minutes? How much will they speak or write?
- Why use AI here? What would be lost without it?
- Is the tool allowed for your learners' age, and who uses it — you or them?
- How will you know the lesson worked?
- What happens if the internet or the tool fails?
- Which theory explains why your activity should help?

## Questions for research pitches
kind: questions
- What is your research question in one sentence?
- Who are the participants, how many, and can you really reach them before December?
- What exactly will you measure, and with what instrument?
- Quantitative or qualitative — and why that one?
- What would count as an interesting result? What would count as a disappointing one?
- How is this related to digital materials or AI in language teaching?
- What is the smallest version of this study you could finish?
- How will you get consent, especially if participants are under 18?
- Which five sources will your literature review start from?

## Giving useful feedback
kind: card
- **Be specific:** "Who are the Grade 5 learners — one class or several?" helps more than "Interesting idea!"
- **One question, one suggestion:** ask about the biggest gap, then offer one thing to try.
- **Feedback from people on the other track is valuable** — a teaching-demo person sees research ideas from the classroom, and a researcher asks for evidence.
- Write it down, so the speaker can use it later — it belongs in their portfolio.

## Exit ticket
kind: checklist
Submit before you leave — one per group, or one each if you work alone.
- Track: research or teaching demo
- Group members, or *individual project*
- Working title
- Target learners in one line
- One question you must answer before the proposal deadline (11/04)
